## Lessons Carried Forward

- **A stop-loss threshold of -250% on a short put is not a stop — it is a catastrophe acknowledgment.** KTOS at -852% and AADX at -9,100% both fired "correctly" by config and both produced unacceptable losses. The threshold must be set relative to the maximum acceptable dollar loss per position, not as a percentage of premium received. A $0.05 premium with a -250% stop allows a $0.125 option value before closing — on a name like AADX, that is one adverse intraday tick away from a 9,000% loss.

- **The `no_auto_manage` list must never be used to exempt a losing position from stops.** The June 16 commit excluded FJET from trailing stops and ladder management. The intent was to prevent runaway ladder-buying on IPOs. The effect was to strand a deteriorating position with no exit path. Exemption from *one* management tool cannot mean exemption from *all* exit logic.

- **The wheel must read current book P&L before firing.** The AM/PM double-fire on 6/29, the contradictory open/close on 6/22, and the same-session ALB open/close on 6/26 all have a common cause: the wheel strategy evaluates opportunity independently of what the position_manager is simultaneously doing. These are not edge cases — they occurred in 3 of 4 active trading days in W26–W27.

- **Adding tickers to the wheel matrix without pre-trade analysis requirements is directly dangerous.** FJET and AADX were committed into the matrix on 6/12. Both became the worst positions of the month within two weeks. The matrix addition process must require a pre-trade record in `strategy_analysis` before the first order can be generated.

- **An 11.5% win rate with a profit factor of 0.23 is not bad luck.** The average loss ($284.39) exceeds the average win ($495.33) in gross terms, but the win count (3) versus loss count (23) dominates the expectancy. The system is not selecting entries that have a positive expected value — it is mechanically firing the wheel into names with directional risk (uranium complex, high-beta small caps, zero-day IPOs) without regime or fundamental filters that would screen them out.
