Two diagnoses were wrong

The quarterly was written from journal narrative, not from the source. Two of its five root causes did not survive contact with the code, and in both cases the wrong mechanism would have produced the wrong fix — a change that looks responsive and leaves the actual failure running.

It said the stop-loss monitor ran once daily. It runs every 60 seconds. The real fault was elsewhere and worse: the stop fired correctly and then a resting order muted the position for the rest of the day.

It proposed gating entries on credit / max_loss_at_stop. With a percentage-based stop that ratio is C / (2.5 × C) — exactly 0.4 for every trade ever placed. It can never reject anything. Shipping it would have added a gate that does nothing and closed the finding.

The five causes

Ranked by expected impact on the equity curve, in the quarterly's own order. Each card reads: what the review said, what the code actually did, and what changed.

01

Equity longs had no exit at all

Diagnosed

FJET left unmanaged for six consecutive weeks; no stop-loss parameter exists for equity positions.

Actually

Correct, and the cause is a fix from June. no_auto_manage stopped the ladder averaging into FJET by dropping those tickers out of sync_positions() entirely — which also removed their trailing stop. Suppressing the ladder silently suppressed the exit, so FJET fell to −32% with nothing watching it.

Fixed

A 25% loss floor that reads live broker positions, so quarantined names cannot hide from it. Long equity only, fires once per ticker via guards.acted_once. It also cancels our own resting sells first — shares locked by a working order aren't available, so the liquidation would have been rejected and appeared to fire.

02

A resting close order muted its own position

Diagnosed

The monitor checks marks at most once daily; poll every 30 minutes instead.

Actually

Wrong. run_cycle() already runs every 60 seconds. The KTOS stop did fire at −250%. Its marketable limit at ask × 1.03 never filled, and the "already has a working order" guard then skipped that symbol every cycle for the rest of the day. The loss ran on to −852% behind a guard that was right about double-submission and wrong about duration.

Fixed

Working orders are aged. Past stale_order_seconds the order is cancelled and re-priced, crossing the spread harder each attempt (3% → 5% → 7%), bounded by max_reprice_attempts and then alerting rather than chasing forever. Re-submitting the price that already failed to fill was the bug.

03

The wheel never consulted the book

Diagnosed

The wheel reads a universe list, not a live ledger, so it opens into an already-stressed book.

Actually

Confirmed. v2.1 shipped position_ledger for exactly this coordination and the wheel was never wired to it — so on one morning it sold six new CSPs while the position manager was closing and rolling the same names.

Fixed

Two gates fed from live positions: a cycle-level max_book_loss_pct (15%), because a stressed book is a property of the book rather than of any ticker, and a per-name skip_losing_underlying — being short a put is already bullish, and adding another while the first is underwater doubles a thesis the market is actively disagreeing with.

04

Every strike was the same distance out, on every name

Diagnosed

No minimum expected value on entry; require credit / max_loss_at_stop > 0.15.

Actually

The proposed formula is a constant — it rejects nothing. The real defect is upstream: select_csp_strike() placed every name at the same ~6.25% OTM regardless of volatility. That is far away on a quiet name and inside a normal fortnight on a volatile one.

Fixed

Both the gate and the selector now measure against the underlying's own 1-sigma move over the holding period. Gating alone would have had the selector propose a too-close strike forever and the gate reject it — replacing a losing wheel with an idle one. Both fail open when vol is unavailable, so this can't become a second silent IV gate.

05

The policy classifier matched substrings

Diagnosed

Sector mapping mis-classifies aviation, agricultural, automotive and chemical policy as AI-infrastructure; audit the mappings.

Actually

Not a mapping problem — a matching problem, in one line. Keywords were tested with kw in text. "ai" matched AIrcraft, dairy, remain; "ice" matched price, service, Office. That is the whole explanation for five weeks of "classifier artifact" entries.

Fixed

Whole-word matching with an optional plural. The fix needed a fix: plain \b would have stopped "tariffs" matching keyword "tariff" and quietly dropped real signal while cleaning up the false positives.

The FJET decision

The 25% floor would have liquidated FJET on deploy at −32.3%. You chose to keep it and write covered calls instead, so the position is exempt — with its reasoning recorded in the config, not just in a commit message.

Keep the position, write the calls

  • Exempt, on the record. catastrophic_exempt carries the plan — keep the GTC breakeven sell at $5.71 rather than realize −$8,420 today. Anything on that list must state a plan, and comes off when the plan ends.
  • Covered calls were never reachable. run_cycle() only ever called open_csp(); open_cc() could only be reached from handle_assignment(). No call was ever written against a holding acquired any other way — which is why FJET read "CC eligible" for six weeks and nothing happened.
  • Sized to 309, not 4,570. The other 4,261 shares are locked by the breakeven order, so the call is 3 contracts. Sizing off held shares would have submitted a call we cannot cover.
  • Struck off spot, not basis. A basis-derived strike lands at $5.81 against a $3.86 spot — ~50% out, bidding roughly nothing, which is exactly why August 7th concluded "write no CCs". The spot-based strike is $4.50. The trade-off is explicit: it caps recovery below cost basis.
  • And it can't stack. sync_positions() never detected short calls, so a CC-writing loop would have sold another one every cycle — the ladder-runaway failure again, with different orders.
